Which samples can be trusted

Each sample is judged against the thresholds below, which you can change here: every panel re-flags at once, while the pipeline's own gate and qc_flags.tsv keep the defaults. Samples that fail start in the exclusion list; review the rest and export the list for the downstream analysis.

3 · Genome & genes

Completeness of each consensus, where along the reference calls are missing, reads are absent or variants concentrate, and which genes carry them.

4 · Relatedness

How close the samples are to each other

SNP differences between the consensus sequences, counted only where both samples called a base, so a gap or a masked position is never a difference. These are the distances a tree built on the consensus sequences sees. Samples mapped against different references are not compared.

Distances every pair of samples on a reference, in the order that keeps each cluster together; move the threshold to redraw the clusters

Clusters samples joined by a chain of pairs within the threshold

Outside their group samples far from the rest of their own patient, line or series

5 · Variants over time

How allele frequencies change along each series

Every variant followed through the time points of its series (a patient, a passage line), the pairs that move together, and the full site × sample matrix.

Gained along each series fixed SNPs each sample carries that the first time point of its series did not

Minority variants how low an allele frequency can be trusted: the calls below fixation and, with libraries of the same DNA, how often another library calls them too

SNP dynamics allele-frequency trajectories per gene; the screen at the top ranks what is changing

Co-varying pairs variants whose trajectories move together (concordant) or against each other (discordant) within a series

SNP matrix every SNP site × sample, as allele frequency; filter by gene or position and download the full matrix

Variant × dose per-variant allele frequency against dose (Spearman ρ with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR); click a row to plot it

6 · Resistance

Resistance-associated mutations

Mutations in the WHO catalogue called by pathotypr straight from the reads (H37Rv numbering). Those every sample of a lineage shares are separated from those only some samples carry. A genomic screen, not a drug-susceptibility result.

Resistance mutations grade 1–2 = associated with resistance, 3 = uncertain, 4–5 = not associated

Resistance along each series the resistance mutations at every time point, those acquired since the first one set apart

7 · Gene conversion

Candidate gene conversion between paralogs

Stretches where one copy of a gene family seems to have been overwritten by another, and the evidence that decides whether to believe each one: an ordinary substitution matching the paralog and reads that came from the donor produce the same bases, and the Bayes factor (BF) says how much better a conversion explains the reads.

Candidate tracts one row per event; filter by verdict, search a sample, locus or donor

8 · Diagnostics

How the QC metrics relate

Views for digging into a problem once you have seen it elsewhere. None of them flags a sample on its own.

Metric pairs any two metrics against each other; drag a box to add the enclosed samples to the exclusion list vs

Correlation matrix Spearman rank correlation between the core metrics over the samples in view; click a cell to plot that pair above

QC space samples placed by their whole QC profile (PCA on robust z-scores); a map, not a test and not a phylogeny

Most unusual samples (robust Mahalanobis rank + top deviating metrics)

Divergence vs completeness separates reference-bias suspects from honest low coverage; a screen to inspect, not a flag

Dose × treatment dose per treatment group and a Kruskal–Wallis test of whether it differs between groups
